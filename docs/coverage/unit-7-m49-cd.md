# Unit 7 — Module 49 Legal Issues: Exemplars 49.C and 49.D coverage

Sources: Legal Issues PowerPoint (Module 49), slides 22–27 (with [Notes]); related slides 2 and 5; Module 49 study guide (Module_49_Legal_Issues_Study_Guide-v2), pp. 2828–2830 and 2834 (PDF pages 3–4, 8–9).

## Summary

- **Items inventoried:** 54 (slides 22–27: every bullet, list item, and speaker note; related slide 2 objective and slide 5 invasion of privacy; study guide HIPAA, Communication concept row, and child-abuse reporting items).
- **Before:** no exemplar lessons for 49.C or 49.D. HIPAA basics and child-abuse reporting were taught briefly inside `law-torts-privacy` and `consent-laws-lifespan` (guides-m49-pro.js). HIPAA's insurance purposes, privacy vs. confidentiality, the three confidentiality actions, the Security Rule, breaches, social media, the reporting chain, good faith immunity, injury/disease reporting, and NPA-violation reporting were missing or thin.
- **Added:** 2 new lessons (14 sections each, about 3,800–3,900 words, vg.mjs OK, grade ~5–6): `privacy-confidentiality` (Exemplar "49.C Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act") and `mandatory-reporting` (Exemplar "49.D Mandatory Reporting"). 31 flashcards (16 + 15); every slide key term has one. No questions written.
- **North Carolina:** G.S. 7B-301 universal child reporting to county DSS (failure is a crime), G.S. 108A-102 disabled adults to county DSS, good-faith immunity, communicable disease to local health department, NCBON reporting of conduct issues and alternative-to-discipline programs, Just Culture tool, Compact state, G.S. 90-21.5 minor consent. Consistent with guides-m49-pro.js.
- **Course-vs-current notes (5):**
  1. HIPAA "reduces barriers related to preexisting conditions" — true, but since 2014 the ACA bars most plans from excluding preexisting conditions.
  2. Slide 24's permitted-sharing list (client, providers, payers) is correct but incomplete — HIPAA also allows operations, family involved in care if the client doesn't object, and reports required by law.
  3. Slide 26 reporting "through the administrative chain" — use the chain, but the report must reach the agency; in NC the child-abuse duty is personal (matches the instructor's slide note).
  4. Slide 27 lists stab wounds and poisonings with gunshot wounds — gunshot reporting is required in most states; other injuries vary by state and situation (matches the slide note).
  5. Study guide: failing to report child abuse "may result in civil charges" — in NC and many states it is also a crime (same note as guides-m49-pro.js). The study guide's Fourth Amendment privacy note is repeated consistently.
