## Clinical clarifications

These are explicitly identified in the guides rather than silently presented as exact slide wording. References checked October 27, 2026.

- **Pediatric oral care:** the slides suggest water-only brushing at 18 months and a six-month first dental visit. [AAPD parent guidance](https://www.aapd.org/resources/parent/faq/) and [AAPD periodicity guidance](https://www.aapd.org/globalassets/media/policies_guidelines/bp_recdentperiodschedule25.pdf) support a tiny fluoride smear from the first tooth under age three, a pea-sized amount at ages three to six, and a dental home when the first tooth erupts and no later than age one. Question m7-u6-017 tests the identified clinical update.
- **Sleep:** [NHLBI](https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep/stages-of-sleep) uses N1, N2, N3 rather than the older N3/N4 split. Cycle timing varies. [NINDS sleep guidance](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2025-05/understanding-sleep.pdf) supports sleep-promoting GABA pathways; the slide’s reduced-GABA implication is not repeated. Question m7-u6-025 uses current staging.
- **Activity:** weekly totals are distinguished from a universal daily prescription. [CDC activity guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/physical-activity-basics/guidelines/index.html) supports age-specific targets. Isokinetic exercise is defined by controlled constant angular speed; an ordinary treadmill or bicycle is not automatically an isokinetic device.
- **Delirium:** the deck’s medication list is not used as a routine algorithm. [NICE recommendations](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg103/chapter/Recommendations) emphasize cause assessment, supportive care, de-escalation, and selective short-term medication when indicated after risk review. No doses are supplied. [CAM training guidance](https://americandeliriumsociety.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/CAM-Long_Training-Manual.pdf) supports the classic feature combination; the site does not reproduce a formal screening instrument or imply that a quiz diagnoses delirium.
- **Screens and diagnoses:** cognitive screens and SCOFF prompt evaluation; they do not independently diagnose a cause or establish treatment. Intellectual and developmental assessment includes adaptive function and context, not an isolated IQ cutoff. Descriptions of eating and personality disorders remain concept-level summaries rather than substituted diagnostic criteria.
- **Privacy and records:** a “confidential” subject line is not encryption. Educational record access must be authorized. [HHS guidance](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/minimum-necessary-requirement/index.html) explains the treatment-disclosure exception to minimum necessary. Order-receiving scope, correction steps, and authentication timing follow applicable rules and facility policy, rather than universal claims inferred from a slide.
- **Teaching:** [AHRQ Tool 5](https://www.ahrq.gov/health-literacy/improve/precautions/tool5.html) elaborates teach-back. Preferences are flexible supports; learning is verified by explanation, application, or performance. Watching, signing, or receiving material is not treated as proof of learning.

## Content conventions

- Packs use the existing `window.NURSE_DATA` format and generated JSON pipeline.
- Unit 6 groups m7, m23, m29, m38, and m41; it never replaces m6.
- Guides follow the existing objectives/big-picture/terms/sections/pearls/red-flags/recap/self-check structure.
- Each topic has five original questions and seven flashcards. Flashcards emphasize self-check applications and essential definitions.
- Questions use priority MCQ, single-best-answer MCQ, and SATA. Every option has an explanation; MCQs use parallel wording and varied answer positions. Chart exhibits support selected clinical judgments.
- Regression checks verify module membership, old Module 6 identity, all three formats, source sections, coverage for every topic, valid answer indices, unique keys, and option-rationale alignment.

## Exam-style review and school references

The [2026 NCLEX-RN Test Plan](https://www.nclex.com/files/2026_RN_Test%20Plan_English-F.pdf) and [NCSBN cognitive-level guidance](https://ncsbn.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/5254284273047-At-what-cognitive-level-is-the-NCLEX-written) informed the application/clinical-judgment review. Items are original, course-scoped practice, with clinical scenarios, priority decisions, single best answers, and multiple responses. All six clinical-judgment steps are represented; the selected-response format set follows the existing site. This is not a full NGN format simulation or a psychometrically calibrated examination. Difficulty tags are editorial estimates, and no official NCLEX or school endorsement is claimed.

A separate QA agent independently reviewed keys, distractors, clinical content, source coverage, tags, and site interactions. Revisions removed answer-length cues, replaced several label-recall/source-aware stems with clinical applications, refined Client Needs categories by item, and completed the factual documentation example. Each guide also includes an original worked bedside case with reasoning and an applied self-check.
